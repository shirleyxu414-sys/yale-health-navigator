The problem: where do I even call?

  • Who: every Yale student, staff or faculty member, dependent and visiting student who needs care
  • Yale Health has many doors: Member Services, Acute Care, primary care, PTSMC, mental health
  • The rules depend on who you are, and the answer to one question sits on several pages and 100+ page PDFs
  • The phone line that can confirm it closes at 5 p.m. and on weekends
  • Cost: time, frustration, wrong first calls, visits without a referral

How the agents work

Router, coverage checker and plan builder are one gpt-6-luna call each. Code screens before and verifies after. The chat agent uses a lookup tool.

Real run: "lower back pain for weeks, I want physical therapy" gives the main line, PCP referral first, covered 100% only with it. Right: the chat quoting its rule.

Safety, built in code

  • Emergency: only "Call 911", nothing else
  • Injection: 5 of 5 attempts refused, no model call
  • Diagnosis: refused, sent to a clinician
  • Invented facts: quote and numbers checked, else "I don't know"
  • Overspending: 200,000 tokens per session

495 tests pass, including the injection test and the budget guard.

Impact

  • A full plan in 8 to 12 seconds, for about a sixth of a cent
  • Available at any hour; Member Services is Mon to Fri, 8:30 to 5:00
  • 4 roles, 16 to 18 referral departments, every answer cited

Honest limit: I have not tested it with a real user yet, so there is no measured time saving. Next: a 5 to 10 person test with timings and quotes.

Limits and what is next

  • Thin rule files for dependents and visiting students: many questions go to Member Services
  • Strict grounding can wrongly say "I don't know" when a number is rephrased
  • Runs locally, not hosted. Prompt caching not used yet (0 cached tokens)

Next: real-user test, host it, more rules with sources, turn on prompt caching
